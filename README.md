## Overview

The **Computational Time Machine (CTM)** is a research framework for testing whether technological R&D progress can be accelerated by a persistent, generational AI research ecosystem rather than by parallelizing isolated research attempts alone.

Its central mechanism is **Civilization-in-the-Loop (CitL)**: independently validated discoveries may modify the tools, models, simulators, workflows, experimental capabilities, and engineering infrastructure inherited by successor research generations. The resulting system is evaluated as a causal, cumulative R&D process rather than merely as a larger agent population.

Version 2.0 develops CTM into a measurement and experimental framework. It formalizes the **Technological Time Compression Ratio (TTCR)**, introduces **Causal Inheritance Depth (CID)**, specifies controlled baselines and ablations, and adds compute, energy, provenance, physical-validation, safety, and falsification requirements.

## Core thesis

> The meaningful “time machine” is not transport into the future; it is the ability to compute and validate technological futures faster than chronological civilization can reach them.
